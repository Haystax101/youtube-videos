# Idea bank (with fact seeds)

Each idea carries the fact that makes it worth a video, a suggested ending and a style. The
seeds are starting points from memory: **verify every fact and add a source in
`experiment.facts` before scripting**. Move an idea to `episodes/LOG.md` when used. No
poo/toilet topics. Aim for a daily mix: one beneficial or survivable idea, one absurd event,
and one life change or true-history story.

Ending key: **D** dies, **S** survives (often with a weird consequence), **B** better off.

## Beneficial habits (george: "healthy or beneficial things too")

- **Ice bath every day** (B, lowpoly). The day-1 gasp (cold shock) drowns more people in icy water
  than hypothermia; after ~5 dips it's about halved. Myth: straight after the gym it blunts muscle
  growth. Payoff: fall through ice → don't swim, float a minute (1-10-1 rule). *Sample written.*
- **Sauna every day** (B, lowpoly). Finnish study of ~2,300 men: 4-7 sessions a week linked to ~40%
  lower death rate than once a week; heart rate climbs like a brisk walk. Myth: you don't "sweat out
  toxins", sweat is ~99% water. Catch: sauna + alcohol is how Finns die in them.
- **10,000 steps every day** (B). The number came from a 1965 Japanese pedometer ad ("manpo-kei",
  10,000-step meter). Death-risk benefit levels off around 7-8k steps for most adults.
- **Eating only potatoes for a year** (B/S). Andrew Taylor did it in 2016 and lost ~50 kg. Potatoes
  have vitamin C; the Irish famine shows what happens when they're gone.
- **Eating a kilo of carrots a day** (S, funny). Carotenemia: your skin turns orange, harmlessly,
  palms first. Myth: carrots don't give night vision (WW2 British propaganda to hide radar).
- **Face in cold water every morning** (B). The diving reflex: cold water on the face slows the
  heart by 10-25%; it's why free divers hold their breath for minutes.
- **Morning sunlight every day** (B). Light in the eyes sets your body clock; you get sleepy earlier
  at night.

## Absurd single events (survivable ones are the surprise)

- **Struck by lightning** (S, lowpoly). ~90% survive; most current flashes over the skin; it can blow
  your shoes off; the heart often restarts but breathing doesn't, so CPR saves you; myth: victims
  aren't electric. Red fern scars (Lichtenberg figures). Roy Sullivan, struck 7 times. *Sample written.*
- **Falling 10 km without a parachute** (S). Vesna Vulović, flight attendant, survived a 10,160 m fall
  in 1972 (Guinness record). Terminal velocity ~200 km/h within ~12 s.
- **Swallowed by a whale** (S). 2021, lobster diver Michael Packard spent ~30-40 s in a humpback's
  mouth and was spat out: a humpback's throat is too narrow to swallow a person.
- **Exposed to space without a suit** (S). ~15 s of consciousness; your blood doesn't boil; in 1966 a
  NASA test subject (Jim LeBlanc) felt the saliva bubble on his tongue, and woke up fine.
- **Falling into quicksand** (S). Myth: you can't be sucked under; you're less dense than quicksand
  and float about waist deep. Wiggle your legs slowly to let water in.
- **Penny dropped from the Empire State Building** (S, myth-bust). It tumbles, tops out around
  40-50 km/h, and stings.
- **Bitten by a rattlesnake** (S). Myth: never suck out the venom or use a tourniquet. Antivenom is made
  in horses or sheep.
- **Locked in a walk-in freezer** (D or S). Hypothermia stages; "paradoxical undressing": many victims
  strip off at the end because their blood vessels suddenly dilate and they feel hot.
- **Drinking only sea water when stranded** (D). Your kidneys can't make urine saltier than sea water,
  so every sip costs more water than it gives.
- **Swallowing chewing gum** (S, myth). It doesn't stay 7 years; it's out in a few days.
- **Eating the world's hottest chilli** (S). A 2018 BMJ case report: a man got "thunderclap" headaches
  after a Carolina Reaper as arteries in his brain clamped down. Milk helps (casein), water doesn't.
- **Cutting off a finger** (S, useful). Wrap it in damp gauze, in a bag, on ice (not directly on ice);
  it can be reattached hours later.

## Life changes (die only if the route has a turn)

- **Headphones at max volume all day** (S). 85 dB is safe for ~8 hours; at 100 dB, about 15 minutes.
  Tinnitus first; hair cells never grow back.
- **Never showering** (S). You don't die: skin microbes reach a new balance; risk is a crusty patch
  doctors call dermatitis neglecta.
- **Never brushing your teeth** (D, slow). Gum disease bacteria are found in heart artery plaques.
- **Eating polar bear liver** (D/S, also storybook). Hypervitaminosis A: skin peels off in sheets.
  Arctic explorers learned the hard way.
- **Only fast food, every meal** (S). Morgan Spurlock's 30 days: +11 kg and a fatty liver.

## True-history stories (low-poly now that style is locked: goal, stake, joke, turn)

- **Eiffel Tower parachute, 1912** (D). Franz Reichelt, tailor; a cash prize for an aviator's parachute;
  told police he'd use a dummy; filmed by Pathé; turn: months later a Russian actor, Gleb Kotelnikov,
  proved the backpack parachute by opening it behind a speeding car. *Rewrite written.*
- **Mawson's Antarctic trek, 1912** (S). Alone after both companions died; ate his sled dogs; the dog
  livers (vitamin A) likely poisoned Mertz; Mawson's soles came off and he tied them back on.
- **The dancing plague, Strasbourg 1518** (D/S). Hundreds danced for days; the city hired musicians
  and built a stage to "dance it out", which made it worse.
- **The Great Molasses Flood, Boston 1919** (D/S). A tank burst; a wave of molasses several metres high
  swept the streets at ~50 km/h; 21 died. Cold January made it set like glue.
- **Phineas Gage, 1848** (S). An iron rod through his skull; he sat up and talked; his personality changed.
- **Violet Jessop** (S). Survived the Olympic collision, the Titanic and the Britannic sinking.
- **The Great Emu War, 1932** (S, funny). Australia sent soldiers with machine guns against emus. The emus won.
- **Charles Joughin, Titanic's chief baker** (S). Said he drank whisky and paddled for hours in water that
  killed others in minutes.
